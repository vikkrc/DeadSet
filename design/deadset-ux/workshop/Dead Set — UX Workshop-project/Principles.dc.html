The questions, answered

Four tests every screen, scene and setting in Dead Set should pass. Part II of the guide applies them to the lobby, the rule book, the Gazette and the rest.

What counts as good UI and UX here

The phone is a private hand; the TV is the table

  • One decision per screen. The move strip names it; the dock confirms it; nothing else asks for a tap.
  • Eyes up, not down. A good phone screen can be read in two seconds and put face down; the game happens between people.
  • Same place, every game. Header, move strip, stage, tray, dock. Learning one game teaches the controls of all eleven.
  • Recover from anything. A dropped phone, a late friend, a host who leaves: every state has a way back.
What makes it feel alive and fun

Anticipation, consequence, recognition

  • Anticipation: reveals that take a breath (a held beat before a card turns, beads landing one by one, a heartbeat at ten seconds).
  • Consequence: every action leaves a mark: a crack, a thread of suspicion, a token flying to the ledger.
  • Recognition: your face on the table, your name in the Gazette's headline, an award for your best moment, the evening's ledger.
  • Life at rest: a candle that flickers, a speaker spotlight, faces that react when accused. Small, slow, never in the way.
The right amount of information

A ladder of five, from scene to whisper

  • Interrupt (full scene) only when the game changes; Announce (banner) for a turn; Inform (move strip, caption) for what to do; Ambient (ring, ledger) for state; Private (slip) for your secret.
  • Each piece of news appears once, at the right rung, and leaves a trace in the Gazette for anyone who missed it.
  • The TV tells the room; the phone tells you. Never make six people read the same paragraph on six phones.
Easy, but not bland

Plain controls, rich world

  • Controls are plain: Garamond labels, one action, big targets. The world is rich: blackletter, vellum, wax, etchings, music.
  • Defaults that play well: a recommended setup per player count, so a new host never needs the settings.
  • Depth on request: rules a tap away, customisation one level down, the full record in the Gazette.
  • Character in the copy: "Bram did not wake" beats "Player eliminated". Clear first, then flavour.
