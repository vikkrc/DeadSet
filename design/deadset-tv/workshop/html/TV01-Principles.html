The ten-foot rules

The TV is the table everyone shares. It is read from across the room, in glances, by people who are talking. Every board in this workshop follows these rules.

Three zones of attention
the stage

Hero

The table, wheel, ball, cemetery, dilemma. 60–70% of the screen.

the band and the seats

Pulse

Whose turn, who has acted, time left. Changes every few seconds; glanceable.

the ledger

Record

Scores, the team, the last three headlines. Changes slowly; for whoever looks.

1One hero at a time. The stage shows the thing the room is deciding about, as large as the screen allows: the table 680–760 px, the wheel 640, a dilemma card 560 wide. Panels never compete with it.
2Read from the sofa. 22 px is the floor (≈ 26 px on a 1080p TV, readable at 3 m). Body 24, herald 30, names on seats 17 in small caps with 0.16 em tracking. The log lives on phones; the TV shows headlines.
3State over sentences. Who has acted is a gilt tick on their seat and a bead in the band, not “7 of 8 done” in the corner. Every status the room asks out loud (“who hasn’t voted?”) must be answerable from the seats alone.
4The TV never needs a reader. Nobody operates the TV. No buttons beyond the score bell; the score prompt appears once in the lobby. Nothing on it waits for a tap.
5Secrets never reach the TV. Only public state. Night screens show sleep, not choices; the reveal waits for the server’s reveal time.
6Same places, every game. Header, stage, ledger, band: the clock is always top right, the herald always in the band, the join code always bottom right.
