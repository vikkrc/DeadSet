1Join is the heroUntil the table fills, the QR (250 px on vellum) and the code (38 px tiles) are the biggest things on screen, with the real address. The band repeats the one instruction in 30 px. The TV hides its own join chip in the band while this panel is up.
2Empty chairsThe table shows seats for the players expected (the preset’s count, or 6 by default) as dashed chairs, so the room sees the shape of the game before anyone sits. A chair fills the moment a phone joins.
3ArrivalEach new player’s portrait drops onto the next chair (Deal, 450 ms) with a soft chime; their name types in. Choosing a face shows a gilt halo on the seat (here: Ilka); ready shows the tick.
4What we play, largeThe cover at 130 px (art only; the name is beside it) with the title (34 px), pitch, player range and length: the room knows what it signed up for. It changes live as the host browses games on their phone.
5Teach, then tonight’s cardsThe teach step cycles every 8 s (UX Part II, B1). Under it, a slow upward ticker of every card and rule in play: card, name, one line of what it does; rules first, in rubric. It re-deals when the host changes the deck.
6No buttonsNo “Start the score”, no “Press F”. The score starts with the host’s first tap; the full-screen hint shows once, for 5 s, in the band.
